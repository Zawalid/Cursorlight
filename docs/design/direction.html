Beacon, design direction, draft 1

Your cursor is the product. So the window is a stage for it.

Beacon's whole job is light: finding, pointing, marking. The direction below makes the app itself behave like that. A live miniature of your screen sits at the top of every page, your real pointer plays across it, and every setting changes it as you drag. Nothing is described that can be shown.

01 The idea

Not a settings window. A small control room with a stage.

The Stage is the hero

The top 40% of the window is a live miniature screen. Move your mouse over it and your cursor gets the highlight you are configuring. No fake animation loop, no static screenshots.

Light is the only decoration

The interface is calm and quiet so the effects are the loudest thing in it. One warm color, Ember, is allowed to glow. It marks what is on, selected or live, and nothing else.

The keyboard is a feature

Shortcuts are big keycaps that really press when you press the real keys. Triggers are something you try on the page, not a table you read.

What this is not: a sidebar of flat cards, a laptop illustration, a gradient hero, or anything that looks like another app. The pieces are borrowed from different places (see the last section) and recombined around one idea.

02 Color: three kinds of light

Night, Daylight, Dusk. One accent, three moods.

You choose a "light" instead of a theme. Each is a full finish for the window, the stage and the popover. Ember is the same hue in all three and is used for light only: selected tiles, beams, the on state, the primary button.

Rules

Text never below 4.5:1. Ember never carries text smaller than 14px (it glows, it does not label). Surfaces are solid except in Dusk. No gradient fills on chrome: gradients exist only as light.

Effect colors

Each highlight style keeps its own signature (iris, amber, ember, blue, teal) so previews stay recognisable, plus white and a high-contrast yellow. These are content, not chrome.

Status colors

Go is green, caution is amber. Only the Signal (share status) and permissions use them, and always with words, never color alone.

04 Layout

Stage on top, tray below, a dock to move around.

No sidebar and no tab bar. The stage never goes away; the dock changes what the stage shows and what the tray offers. Scenes (Stream, Meeting, Recording) live up top because they change everything at once. Try it: the stage follows your mouse, the finish switch re-lights the whole window, the keycaps press when you hold Ctrl and Option and tap H, L or I.

What the dock changes

Look: the stage runs the highlight. Draw: the stage becomes a canvas, scribble a circle and it snaps. Keys: the stage becomes a keyboard that lights as you press. Apps: the stage shows the app icons and their scenes. Screens: the stage shows your real displays.

Why not a sidebar

A sidebar makes every page a list of settings. Here only five things exist, each with a place where it is shown working. About and updates live in a small footer menu, not a page.

Sizes

Window 980 by 700, resizable down to 860 by 600. Stage 41% of height. 18px outer margin, 14px between panes, 16px pane radius, 20px window radius. Controls 8px radius, tiles 12px.

05 Components

A short kit, all of it made of light.

Aura
Lens

Style tile

A live thumbnail, not an icon: it animates on hover and runs the real recipe when selected. Selected state is a lit edge.

Beam slider

The track fills with a trail of light that ends in a glowing head. It is the cursor comet as a control. Drags are springy, release settles with no overshoot.

CtrlOptH

Keycap

A physical key: a flat top, a dark underside. It presses and glows when the matching real key goes down, anywhere in the app.

Scene chips

A pill group at the top of the window. Switching a scene re-lights the stage in one spring. Long-press to rename.

Whole screen shared
One window: no effects

Signal

The share status as a small beacon: lit and warm when viewers can see effects, amber when they cannot. Always with words. Click to say what you are sharing.

Switch and dock

Native-feeling switch, ember when on. The dock is a floating pill that sits over the tray, like a real Dock, and shrinks to icons when the window is narrow.

Consequence cards

For choices with side effects (how long ink stays, how bold the laser is), three cards with one line each: Subtle, nothing lingers. Balanced, fades in two seconds. Dramatic, trails stay and glow. Taken from LookAway.

Real controls where they exist

Pop-up menus, text fields, the file picker and the permission buttons stay native, so Beacon feels like it belongs on the Mac. Only the three things that are about light (slider, tile, signal) are custom.

06 Illustration

One motif, a ring of light on simple shapes.

No mascots and no laptops. Illustrations are tiny abstract screens (rounded rectangles with a few bars) with one ring or beam of Ember light doing something. Two tones plus the glow, thin 1.5px strokes, drawn in code so they follow the finish and can animate.

Find

A pointer inside a ring of light on a dimmed screen. Used for Look and the first onboarding step.

Mark up

A shaky dotted stroke and the clean ellipse it becomes. Used for Draw and the second onboarding step.

Share

Your screen, a path of light, their screen. The ring shows on theirs only if you share the whole screen. Used for the Signal and Share-safe moments.

Keys

The Keys page draws a plain row of keycaps in the finish color. Ctrl, Opt, Shift and Cmd are always there and light up when held; the chosen shortcut keys glow Ember.

Permissions

Never a lock or shield. A tiny diagram of the real System Settings switch, off then on, with the sentence that says what stays private.

Empty states

One small ring, one sentence, one button. "No app rules yet. Add Zoom and Beacon changes scene when it is in front."

07 Onboarding

Three beats, and you do each one.

A single window, 640 by 460, same stage language. Every step has you perform the thing on a live stage; the Continue button wakes up only after you did it (and is always skippable). No cards, no step bars, three small dots.

How it works

Three big round buttons, each a tiny lit ring that glows when its effect is on, with the shortcut under it. Scene chips on top, the Signal in the header, a plain footer. 330 wide, system popover material, no cards inside it. It is the one place where Beacon must be understood in one second.

Right click on the icon gives the short menu (Settings, Pause, Quit). The icon tints amber when the Signal needs attention.

09 Motion

Springs, never fades and never bounces.

One spring

Everything uses the same critically damped spring the cursor effects use (about 250 ms to settle, no overshoot). Tiles, dock, scene changes, sliders. The app moves like the effects it controls.

Light breathes

Glows have a slow breath, a 2.4 second cycle that stops after three, like the Aura. Nothing loops forever, so nothing nags.

Reduced motion

With Reduce Motion on, springs become 120 ms cross-fades, the stage shows a still frame of the effect, and glows stay steady.

10 Where each piece comes from, and your calls

A mix, around one idea of our own.

Borrowed primitives (never looks)

Alcove: the interactive demo as the first screen. Screen Movie: cursor styles shown as a grid with live size. LookAway: consequence cards and drill-in rows. Raycast and Perch: big keycaps and flat privacy copy. Bartender: the absent permission as a feature. Hoy: three-word captions. Edgeline: that a window can have one big illustrated hero and selectable finishes; not its laptop or its tabs. macapp.supply: two-tone headlines.

Ours alone

The Stage that runs your real effect. The dock that changes the stage. Light as the only decoration. The Signal. Keycaps that press for real. Scenes that re-light the whole window.

1. Default light

Night as the default, with Daylight and Dusk as options. Or follow the system appearance automatically?

2. Name

"Light" language (beam, Signal, Stage) works for Beacon. If you rename the app later the whole system survives, except the word itself.

3. Scope of the first build

Build Night first with the Stage, dock, tiles, beam slider and keycaps, then the popover and onboarding, then Daylight and Dusk.
